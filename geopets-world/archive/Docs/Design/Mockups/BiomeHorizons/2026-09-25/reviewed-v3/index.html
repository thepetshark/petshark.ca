Five biomes, five terrain identities

Revision 3 returns to the original terrain and road references: sculpted relief, textured surfaces and grouped modular assets.

Reviewed AI concepts for discussion. Phone performance has not been measured from these scenes.

Meadow — Folded Downs

Overlapping green hills, clustered broadleaf trees and conifers, worn dirt crossings, and a modest buried-stone landmark.

Grass detail should live mainly in terrain color/texture. Existing tree, shrub and rock variants supply clusters; the large slabs can be simple custom meshes.

Grove — Rootfold Hollow

Wooded slopes and gullies framing an open route, with leaf litter, logs, stumps and a small mushroom hollow.

Reuse a small set of tree/undergrowth variants and ground materials. Mushroom undersides are a material accent. Distant vegetation density remains a tuning task.

Stonefield — Weathered Folds

Low rocky ridges, asymmetric humps and shallow grooves across stony ground; shrubs in pockets and one basalt/mineral cluster.

Terrain geometry gives the broad relief. Rock material supplies fine variation. Large off-road boulders and basalt require a small mesh kit; roads follow surface height.

Marsh — Reedbank Hollows

Rounded dry hummocks and irregular shallow pools, grouped reeds and broad leaves, plus mineral-bearing stone fins.

Use calm flat water surfaces, bank geometry and texture variation. Reed/plant/stone clusters are modular. Pool layout in the image is illustrative, not permission to alter real map water.

Snow — Glacial Shoulders

Snowdrifts and snow-capped tree groups beside an interlocking ice shoulder with a crevice.

Snow material and simple cap variants adapt existing props. Ice slabs and crevice edges need a small new mesh kit; the scene does not require volumetric effects.
