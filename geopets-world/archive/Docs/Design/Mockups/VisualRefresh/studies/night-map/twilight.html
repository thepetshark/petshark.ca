Between Moonlit and Blue hour

The reaction to the four night treatments was that Moonlit was fine, but something between Moonlit and Blue hour, with the map keeping more of its colour, would be better. This page turns "between" and "more" into nine levels you can point at, on the game's real map.

Concepts only. Every plate is a game screenshot with a night palette applied by a script, so each level is a set of colours the game could use. Markers are drawn exactly as by day, with no rim or glow: world-space canvases are not lit, so they stay bright on their own.

Lightness of the ground round the player at the default zoom, from 0 for black to 100 for white. The reference night holds roads at 16 and land at 36; this map by day has 44 and 55, a gap of only 11.

The night colours of this level, for whoever builds it:

Default zoomThe distance carries the night; the ground round the player keeps its colour and its contrast.

Fully zoomed inThe sky blends too: a dusk glow on the horizon at the lighter levels, a moon and more stars from half-way on.

Fully zoomed outNo sky and no distance here, so a ground that keeps its colour would read as an overcast day. The edges fall off into the haze colour instead, more strongly the more colour is kept.

What the grid shows

Colour and contrast are separateKeeping the map's colour changes hue only. Every level keeps the lightness the night gives each kind of ground, so roads stay 26 to 30 points darker than land at all nine levels. By day the gap is 11.
The more colour, the less the ground says "night"At the bottom row the grass is green, the rock grey and the roads brown again. The night is then carried by the distance haze, the sea, the sky and, looking straight down, the fall-off at the edges.
Markers need nothingDrawn as by day, a marker's cream ring has a contrast of 4.4 to 4.9 to 1 against this ground. A graphic needs 3 to 1 to be seen, and by day the same marker has 3.4 to 1.
Lit objects are the open questionA stall, a bush patch, a pickup or the player is a lit 3D object, not a canvas. Use the switch at the top: left in the night they go dull and grey, most visibly the stall and the player. Left out of it they read as clearly as the markers.
