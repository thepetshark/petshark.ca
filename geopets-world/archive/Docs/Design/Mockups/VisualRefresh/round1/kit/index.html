Explorer's kit

Built from the things you would take on the walk: a canvas bag, enamel pins, brass, woven labels and label-maker tape, drawn flat like a national-park poster. The game is about going outside to collect; here the collection is a board of pins.

Concept only. Wildling names, rarities, biomes and item titles are real; levels, counts and prices are illustrative. All art on this board was generated for the study. The creature pins are the game's real models translated into pin form: same animals, same shapes.

What makes it this direction

Made of gear, not of panelsSurfaces are canvas flaps with a line of stitching, headers are woven labels, buttons are sewn patches. Flat and graphic, never leathery or grungy.
The collection is a pin boardEvery Wildling is an enamel pin. You do not fill in a list, you fill a board, and an empty mount is a pin you have not earned yet.
Rarity is metal, biome is feltA pin is mounted in bronze, silver or gold, like a medal. The felt behind it is the colour of the biome it lives in.
Trail-marker orange means goOne loud colour, borrowed from waymarks, for the thing to press and for progress. Pack green carries everything else.

Title logo and app icon

Three badges. Geo Pets is the name and WORLD is at least as prominent, in different lettering: an embroidered patch with a stitched globe for the O, an enamel pin, and a routed trail sign. Two app icons keep the selected leaf-eared companion: one as a pin, one as a poster.
