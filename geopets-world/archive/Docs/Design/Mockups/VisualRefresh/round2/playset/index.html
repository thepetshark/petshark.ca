Playset

The world is the smooth toy diorama of the Toybox map from round one, now with rolling ground, a bouldery Stonefield and a sun and a moon that move. The interface is organic and solid: soft blob shapes in flat colour, a friendly rounded face, and not one shadow.

Concept only. The map is the game's real map, redrawn; Wildling names, rarities, biomes, item titles and the confirmation's copy are the game's own; levels, counts, timers and the Hometown level are illustrative. The phone is a modern tall one (412 by 884) with its camera cut-out and gesture bar drawn, and no control sits under either.

What this direction is made of

Colour on colourDepth comes from one flat colour sitting on another. A sheet is cloud white on a dimmed map; a card is its biome's tint on the sheet; a count is coral on anything.
No two blobs alikePanels, cards and buttons have slightly uneven corners, so the interface feels hand-cut and soft without imitating any material.
Things from the worldItem icons are small matte models that could be picked up off the map. They have form, but no gloss, no plate and no cast shadow.
One decorationA small leaf pattern on sheet headers. Nothing else is decorated.

One view, four hours

The same place at the default zoom. Midday is a screenshot of the game, redrawn; the other three are edits of that one image in which only the light changes, so you can watch the sun and the moon move.

DawnThe sun is low on the right, so every tree, boulder and rise throws a long shadow to the left. Mist in the hollows.

MiddayThe master image: the game's real map redrawn in this look. Short shadows, the clearest read of roads and biomes.

Golden hourThe sun has crossed to the left and the shadows now run to the right. This is the hour the other zooms are shown at.

NightThe moon is up. Following the night study, the ground keeps its colour, the distance sinks into haze, and markers are drawn exactly as by day.

The other two zooms, at golden hour

Zooming changes the camera angle, so the light has to work from near the ground and from straight above.

Fully zoomed inThe low camera puts the horizon and the sky in the picture, which is where a low sun does the most.

Fully zoomed outLooking straight down there is no sky: the hour is told by the long shadows and by the lit and shaded sides of the ground.

Hometown, the collection, the inventory and a confirmation

Hometown follows the game's own framing: a close isometric view whose lawn fills the frame, the locked land under cloud that lies on the island, the padlocked portal standing in that cloud. It is always daylight. Finished crafting is delivered automatically, so working buildings show only a timer.

HometownTwo buildings at work, the request board, and the portal to the next area, which opens when your level allows.

WildlingsThe game's creature meshes, unchanged. What marks a rarity and a biome belongs to this direction.

InventoryOne storage meter with an upgrade, as the capacity decision asks; whether materials and goods get separate limits is still open.

A confirmation, at nightTapping outside dismisses it. Shown over the night map to check the interface against the darkest plate.
